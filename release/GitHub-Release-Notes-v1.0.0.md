## Overview

This initial research release introduces the **Structural Intelligence Framework (SIF)**, an open architecture for organizing, reusing, composing, evaluating, and evolving heterogeneous intelligence structures. Rather than assuming that any single model, algorithm, or fixed architecture exhausts the future of intelligence, SIF treats existing methods as a revisable inventory of structural capabilities and future methods as potential additions, adaptations, or replacements.

SIF is presented as a **conceptual research framework, candidate interoperability specifications, and a falsifiable research agenda**. This release does not claim a production implementation, standardized protocol, or experimentally demonstrated general intelligence gains.

## Core Contributions

### 1. Four functional domains

SIF organizes intelligence work into four interacting functional domains—not mutually exclusive software layers:

- **Localization and Intelligence Addressing:** Find the relevant problem structure, context, capability, or computational route, using mechanisms such as LLMs, CCC, MDT, two-phase search, CallingGraphs, relay networks, and world models.
- **Per-Node Intelligence:** Apply diverse algorithms and intelligence capabilities at an addressed node, including LLMs, rules, mathematical optimization, simulation, ANN, and domain-specific tools.
- **Universal Control Planes and Scoring/Folding:** Separate policy and authority, scoring and decision support, and evidence and audit; support multi-perspective evaluation and structural abstraction.
- **Residual-Based Structural Growth:** Diagnose what remains unresolved, propose structural changes, test counter-evidence, and retain, revise, or retire capabilities on the basis of evidence.

### 2. Problem–Capability Dual Maps

The **Structural Problem-Solving Map (PSM)** describes what a problem requires. The **Structural Capability Map (SCM)** describes what the system can offer. **Bidirectional Structural Intelligence Mapping (BSIM)** connects them: problems can lead to capability selection, while new capabilities can reveal previously unrecognized applications and questions.

### 3. Structural Precedent Intelligence

Inspired by the disciplined use of precedents, the **Structural Precedent Registry (SPR)** records reusable structural claims together with their contexts, applicability boundaries, successful and failed cases, evidence, counter-evidence, and lineage. A retrieved precedent is a candidate for adaptation—not proof that it transfers to a new setting.

### 4. Contracts and interoperability

The proposed **Structural Intelligence Contract (SIC)** and **Universal Naming, Typing, and Relay (UNT-R)** mechanisms describe how heterogeneous capabilities can be identified, typed, matched, invoked, and audited without standardizing their internal intelligence algorithms. The framework accommodates exploratory **Portable Intelligence Runtime Pieces (PIRP)**, more formally specified **Portable Intelligence Runtime Units (PIRU)**, and larger composed intelligence structures.

### 5. Adversarial validation and governance

The **Multi-Intelligence Adversarial Sandbox (MIAS)** proposes distinct builder, challenger, evaluator, checker, and simulation roles to test candidate structures and their evaluators. **Structural Counter-Evidence (SCE)** is preserved alongside supporting evidence. Discovery, validation, and action authorization remain separate stages.

### 6. Open-ended structural and perceptual growth

SIF investigates higher-order **Macro-Intelligence Pieces**, collective structural learning, multi-axis scaling, and the **Structural Perception Horizon**—the boundary of structures a system can presently distinguish, represent, and evaluate. Potential worldview expansion and perspective-relative ASI are treated as research hypotheses, not established results.

## Research Status and Boundaries

- **Included:** architecture proposals, conceptual analyses, candidate schemas and APIs, testable hypotheses, and validation plans.
- **Not claimed:** a reference runtime, validated production deployment, completed benchmarks, demonstrated autonomous structural discovery, or universal superiority over alternative AI architectures.
- **Governance principle:** a system may discover a capability without validating it, validate it without authorizing its use, and authorize it only within an explicit and revisable scope.
- **Epistemic principle:** negative results and structural counter-evidence are research outputs, not exceptions to be hidden.

## Next Steps

Priorities following this initial research release include cross-document terminology and schema reconciliation; prototype contract and relay conformance checks; benchmarked precedent retrieval and dual-map matching; adversarial evaluation of structural growth; and controlled studies of macro-structure composition and perception-horizon claims.

These are proposed research directions, not commitments or completed milestones.
